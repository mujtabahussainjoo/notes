## Simple Summary: Three Ways to Ask AI

When you ask an AI to do something, you can give it **no examples**, **one example**, or **a few examples**. These are called **zero-shot**, **one-shot**, and **multi-shot** (or few-shot) prompting. [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/zero-shot-prompting)

***

## The Three Modes in Plain Words

### 1) Zero-shot (No example)
- You just tell the AI what to do.
- Use this when the task is very simple and obvious.

**Example:**
> “Write a short thank-you message for a customer.”

No examples are given. The AI uses what it already knows. [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/zero-shot-prompting)

***

### 2) One-shot (One example)
- You show **one clear example** of what you want, then ask for the same kind of answer.
- Use this when the format or style is important and a short description is not enough.

**Example:**
> Example:  
> Input: “The product arrived late.”  
> Output: “Negative”  
>  
> Now do this:  
> Input: “The product was perfect.”  
> Output: ?

The AI sees the pattern (text → “Positive” or “Negative”) and copies it. [guides.library.tamucc](https://guides.library.tamucc.edu/prompt-engineering/shots)

***

### 3) Multi-shot / Few-shot (Many examples)
- You show **2–5 examples** of input and output.
- Use this when the task has rules, special formats, or tricky cases.

**Example:**
> Example 1:  
> Input: “Great quality!” → Output: “Positive”  
> Example 2:  
> Input: “Not worth the price.” → Output: “Negative”  
> Example 3:  
> Input: “It’s okay, nothing special.” → Output: “Neutral”  
>  
> Now:  
> Input: “I love it, will buy again.” → Output: ?

More examples help the AI understand the pattern better, especially for edge cases. [learnprompting](https://learnprompting.org/docs/basics/few_shot)

***

## Cost vs Quality Trade-off (Simple View)

- **More examples = better quality** (AI understands you more clearly).
- **More examples = higher cost** (each example uses more “tokens,” which is like paying for more words every time). [ppc](https://ppc.land/few-shot-prompting/)

**Rule of thumb:**
- Start with **zero-shot**.
- If the answer is not right, add **one example**.
- If still not right, add **a few examples**.
- Stop when the results are good enough. [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/zero-shot-vs-few-shot-prompting)

***

## How This Works With Different AI Models

- **Stronger models** often do well with **zero-shot**.
- **Smaller/cheaper models** may need **one or more examples** to get the same result. [respan](https://www.respan.ai/articles/few-shot-prompting-vs-zero-shot-prompting)

So you can:
- Try a **cheaper model + a few examples**, or
- Use a **stronger model + no/fewer examples**,  
depending on what your tests show works best. [prompt-architects](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/44-few-shot-vs-zero-shot-prompting)

***

## Tiny Decision Guide (Layman Version)

- Task is simple and obvious? → **Zero-shot**
- Format/style keeps coming wrong? → **One-shot**
- Many rules or special cases? → **Multi-shot (2–5 examples)**

Add only as many examples as needed to get reliable answers, no more. [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/zero-shot-vs-few-shot-prompting)

***

## Simple Diagram (Text)

```
Zero-shot:   [Instruction] → AI answers

One-shot:    [1 Example] + [Instruction] → AI answers

Multi-shot:  [Example 1]
             [Example 2]
             [Example 3] + [Instruction] → AI answers
```

More examples → clearer pattern → better results, but more cost. [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/zero-shot-prompting)
