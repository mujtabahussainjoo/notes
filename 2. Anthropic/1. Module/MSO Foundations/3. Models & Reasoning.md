Here’s a clearer, more “layman” explanation, with no links attached to the text.

***

## Big picture

Claude gives you **two knobs**:

1. **Model choice** – which “size” of Claude you use: Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, or Fable. [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)
2. **Reasoning mode / effort** – how much that model **thinks before answering**. [secondtalent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/every-claude-ai-model-explained-compared/)

You choose the model once for a task, then set how much thinking it should do **for each call**.

***

## Claude model family (four levels)

Think of four phones with different price and power:

- **Haiku** – the budget phone  
  - Fastest and cheapest.  
  - Good for simple, repeated work: tagging, routing, extracting fields, short summaries. [toloka](https://toloka.ai/blog/claude-models-explained/)

- **Sonnet** – the everyday phone  
  - Best balance of speed, cost, and intelligence.  
  - Recommended default for most production apps: coding helpers, RAG chatbots, customer support, normal agents. [academy.claude](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/choosing-the-right-claude-model)

- **Opus** – the “pro” phone  
  - More powerful than Sonnet.  
  - For harder tasks: complex reasoning, big refactors, serious analysis, stronger agents. [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)

- **Fable** – the flagship phone  
  - Most powerful and most expensive.  
  - For the hardest, longest tasks: long‑running agents, high‑risk decisions, very deep reasoning. [secondtalent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/every-claude-ai-model-explained-compared/)

**How to choose in practice:**  
- Start with **Sonnet**.  
- Move **up** (to Opus, then Fable) only when your evaluations show that Sonnet is **missing your quality bar**.  
- Move **down** (to Haiku) only when evaluations say the quality drop is **acceptable** and you want more speed or lower cost. [fast](https://fast.io/resources/claude-model-comparison-guide/)

***

## Reasoning modes (separate from model choice)

Model choice and reasoning mode are **different decisions**: [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking)

- **Model choice** answers: “Which Claude am I using – Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, or Fable?”  
- **Reasoning mode** answers: “How much does this model think before answering this time?”  

Today this is mainly done through:

- **Adaptive thinking**  
  - The model decides **when** and **how deeply** to think based on how hard the question is.  
  - You control intensity with an **effort** setting (for example: low, medium, high), instead of giving a fixed thinking‑token budget. [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking)

- **Old `budget_tokens` control**  
  - Older style where you set a fixed thinking budget.  
  - On newer models this is deprecated and can return a 400 error; you should use adaptive thinking + effort instead. [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/extended-thinking)

- **Thinking visibility**  
  - On new models, detailed internal thinking is usually **hidden by default**.  
  - If you need to show it, you request a **summarized version** of the thinking. [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking)

- **When reasoning is worth it**  
  - It earns its cost on **hard, multi‑step problems**: planning, complex analysis, tricky bugs.  
  - It is mostly wasted on **simple lookups or classification**, where a direct answer is enough. [toloka](https://toloka.ai/blog/claude-models-explained/)

***

## How model and reasoning work together

Because they are independent, you can tune them separately. [toloka](https://toloka.ai/blog/claude-models-explained/)

- **Strong model + low effort**  
  - Example: Opus with low effort.  
  - Fast and direct, good when the task is tough but you still care about latency.  

- **Smaller model + high effort**  
  - Example: Haiku or Sonnet with high effort.  
  - The model spends more tokens thinking, so it is slower but more careful, while still limited by that model’s overall capability. [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking)

- **Hardest tasks**  
  - Use a **capable model** (Opus or Fable) plus **higher effort**, so it can plan, check itself, and handle long chains of reasoning. [fast](https://fast.io/resources/claude-model-comparison-guide/)

Also, defaults differ by model: some newer models think adaptively by default or always, so you should check each model’s default thinking behavior when you build. [platform.claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)

## Simple concrete examples

### Example 1 – Simple labeler

- Task: Read emails and mark them as “billing”, “support”, or “sales”.  
- Choice: **Haiku + low effort**  
- Why: Task is simple; you want low cost and high speed. Deep reasoning is not needed here. [secondtalent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/every-claude-ai-model-explained-compared/)

### Example 2 – General app assistant

- Task: Coding helper or RAG chatbot in your product.  
- Choice: **Sonnet + medium effort**  
- Why: Good default; smart enough for most real‑world problems, still responsive for users. [academy.claude](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/choosing-the-right-claude-model)

### Example 3 – Complex long‑running agent

- Task: Agent that plans and executes a large migration, or performs deep multi‑step analysis.  
- Choice: **Opus or Fable + high effort**  
- Why: Needs maximum reasoning and planning; higher cost is a trade‑off for better safety and quality. [fast](https://fast.io/resources/claude-model-comparison-guide/)

## Diagram: two knobs you control

```text
STEP 1: CHOOSE MODEL  (which Claude?)

   ↑ more capable, slower, more expensive
   │
   │   Fable  → hardest, longest, highest-stakes tasks
   │   Opus   → complex reasoning & coding
   │   Sonnet → default for most production apps
   │   Haiku  → fastest & cheapest for simple work
   └────────────────────────────────────────→

STEP 2: CHOOSE EFFORT  (how much thinking?)

   Low effort   → quick, cheap, shallow thinking
   Medium       → balanced, default level
   High / Max   → deeper thinking, slower, more cost

EXAMPLE COMBOS

   Haiku  + low effort   → instant simple answers
   Sonnet + medium       → everyday production default
   Opus   + high effort  → deep multi-step reasoning
   Fable  + high effort  → toughest long-running agents
```

One sentence to keep in mind:  
**Pick the model based on how hard the job is, then choose effort based on how much real “thinking” that job needs.**
